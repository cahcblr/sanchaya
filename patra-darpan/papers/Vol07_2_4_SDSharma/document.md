According to perturbation theory many inequalities are to be applied to the longitude of the moon, while *Sūrya-siddhānta* (S.S.) applied only one, the equation of the centre, with amplitude, $= 5^\circ$, (approximately). According to S.S. the Minima of all the *tithis* are the same and have a duration of about 54 *ghatis*. The Maxima according to this system are of about 65 *ghatis*. But in fact considering all the important inequalities of the moon it is shown here that the Minima are different for different *tithis*, and the minimum of all the minima of various *tithi* has a duration of about 50 *ghatis*, (in the case of the syzygies (पर्वद्वय) only. The Maxima of all the *tithis* are almost the same of the order of 67 *ghatis*. This analysis exposes many of the discrepancies in the examples quoted through mere suppositions by the followers of the S.S. School.

## INTRODUCTION

Followers of the S.S. School raise objections against the *Dṛk*-school on the basis of the notion of Maxima and Minima of *tithis* in conformity to the *Surya-siddhānta* ( बाण वृद्धिः, रस-क्षयः ). The examples usually quoted by these were put to rigorous mathematical tests on the basis of the perturbation theory. But followers of the S.S. School raised objections about the *Dṛk*-school especially against the maxima and minima predicted by the modern theories. Historically speaking it seems that the first scholar who quoted such examples was Shri Sudhakar Dwivedi. He quoted an example of *ekādaśī* being 50 *ghatis*.[^1] Shri Ketkar did point out suspicion about the probability of such an example,[^2] but the problem was not analysed on the basis of Mathematical approach. This is to be pointed out, that even the followers of the modern school are not cautious about the fact that Minima are different for different *tithi*. This is leading to many unnecessary attempts on their part on the basis of religious texts. For example a believer in the S.S. School, may raise an objection for *ekādaśī* being of 50 *ghatis*, on the basis of *vrata-sannipāt* etc. ( व्रत-सन्निपातः ) and the follower of the Modern school may start looking into the details of religious rules in order to disprove the argumentation, but there is always negligence about the fact that the *tithi ekādaśī* can never be of 50 *ghatis*. ## Theory :—

The longitude of the moon is given by

$$L = \text{The apparent longitude of moon} = \text{longitude of moon} - 6^\circ\text{-}16'\text{-}56\cdot4'' . \text{Sin} (M)$$

$$+ 0^\circ\text{-}12'\text{-}54\cdot7'' . \text{Sin} (2M) - 1^\circ\text{-}20'\text{-}29\cdot5'' \text{ Sin} (2T - M)$$

$$+ 0^\circ\text{-}35'\text{-}41\cdot6'' . \text{Sin} (2T) + 11'\text{-}11\cdot97'' \text{ Sin} (SMK) - 0^\circ\text{-}7' \text{ Sin } \{2(MM - \Omega)\}$$

where
$M = \text{the manda-kendra of moon.}$
$T = \text{the tithi} = \text{the main longitude of moon} - \text{the mean longitude of the sun.}$
and
$MM = \text{the mean longitude of moon,}$
$\Omega = \text{the longitude of Rāhu (the ascending node)}$
$SMK = \text{manda-kendra of the sun.}$
Note that here the *manda-kendra* of the moon is computed from *ucca*.
Note also that
$\text{velocity of tithi} = \text{moon's velocity} - \text{sun's velocity,}$
$\text{velocity of vipat chādya, } (MM - \Omega) = \text{moon's velocity} - \text{Rāhu's velocity.}$
The apparent velocity of the moon is given by

$$V = dL/dt = \text{mean velocity of moon} + 14'\cdot7 \text{ Cos } (2T) - 15'\cdot8 \text{ Cos } (2T - M)$$

$$- 90' \text{ Cos } (M) + 5\cdot7' \text{ Cos } (2M) + 3'\cdot3 \text{ Cos } \{2(MM - \Omega)\}.$$ ... (2)
This can be shown quite easily using the angles in radians. For extremes,

$$dV/dt = 0$$

The solution of the so obtained transcendental equation can be achieved by equating every term to zero. Note that considering all the higher order terms it is not easy to solve the transcendental equation. Retaining only the terms given above we have,

$$\text{Sin } (2T - M) = 0 \qquad \text{i.e. } 2T - M = n_1 \pi$$

$$\text{Sin } (2T) = 0 \qquad \qquad \quad 2T = n_2 \pi$$

$$\text{Sin } (M) = 0 \qquad \qquad \qquad M = n_3 \pi$$

$$\text{Sin } (2M) = 0 \qquad \qquad \quad 2M = n_4 \pi$$

$$\text{Sin } 2(MM - \Omega) = 0 \qquad 2(MM - \Omega) = n_5 \pi$$

and

$$\text{Sin } (SMK) = 0 \qquad \qquad SMK = n_6 \pi$$

(where $n_1, n_2, n_3, \dots, n_6$ are integers).
Since *amāvasyā* and *pūrṇimā* are given by $T = 0$ and $\pi$, respectively, it is clear that only these *tithis* can have minimum duration when the moon is at the *nica* and the sun is at its *ucca*. Note that in fact the maxima-minima lie near $T = 0$ and $\pi$, due to the terms with argument $2T$ and $(2T - M)$ (variation and evection)[^6]; but as the above equations are consistent, these do lie very close to $T = 0, \pi$). The specific value of $n_1, n_2$ etc., for maximal values also can be determined from the condition $d^2V/dt^2 < 0$. The conditions on the position of moon

Under these conditions the only equation of centre of importance will be $\text{Cos } (2T)$ term in the velocity. If we consider

$$T = 12N \text{ (} N \text{ being the number of tithi),}$$

$$(T \text{ in degrees),}$$

then $\text{Cos } (2T) = \text{Cos } (24N)$. The *tithi* will be maximum when the velocity of the moon is minimum and that of the sun maximum. But the velocity of the sun is given by

$$V_s = 3548'' + 120'\cdot5 \text{ Cos } (M_s)$$

where $M_s = \text{The manda-kendra of the sun.}$
When the sun is at the *mandocca* its velocity is maximum $= 61'\cdot8\cdot5''$
When $M = 0 \ (= MM - \Omega)$ then the minimum velocity of moon $=$

$$= 706'\cdot1 - 1\cdot1 \text{ Cos } (24N).$$

(Here approximately the velocity of the moon is taken to be constant for one *tithi*).
It is clear that the maximum durations of all the *tithis* are almost the same equal to about 67 *ghatis*; although there is a slight variation from one *tithi* to the next.
The $N$th *tithi* will have the minimum duration, when the moon has the maximum velocity and the velocity of the sun is the minimum. From the above expression the minimum velocity of the sun $= 57'\text{-}7''$
$\text{For the } N\text{th tithi the maximum velocity of the moon} = 889\cdot6 + 30\cdot5 \text{ Cos } (24N)$

$$\text{Hence the minimum duration of the } N\text{th tithi}$$

$$= \frac{12 \times 60 \times 60}{832\cdot5 + 30\cdot5 \text{ Cos } (24 \ N)} \text{ ghatis.}$$ ... (4)
It is clear that this has the periodicity of 15 *tithis*. From this expression only the *amāvasyā* and *pūrṇimā* come out to have the minimum duration of about 50 *ghatis*. The conditions of such occurrences enable us to pinpoint the timings in the calendar where these minima will occur. The maxima and minima of *tithis* are given in the annexed table.
It is clear that the minimum duration of the 8th *tithi* is about 54 *ghatis*. 4th and 11th *tithis* have almost the same minimum duration of about 52 *ghatis*.

## CONCLUSION

From the above treatment it is clear that the examples quoted by Shri Sudhakar Dwivediji have absolutely no basis at all. The followers of the S.S. School, considering the arguments of Shri Dwivediji to be authoritative, are continuing to put forward the same or similar examples based on that very notion. They also

quote objections about the 9th *tithi*, (*Rām-Navmī*), being of 50 *ghatis*, which is not at all possible in reality. (The minimum duration of the 9th *tithi* is about 54 *ghatis* according to the present analysis). Thus mostly all of the examples being put forward are impossible in reality. According to our survey, unfortunately not even a single example does conform to the perturbation theory. The followers of the S.S. School happened not to give any example for syzygies in order to object to the acceptability of the *Dṛk*-school on the basis of *parva-vrata sannipāt* and *vyatyaya* ( पर्वव्रत सन्निपात, व्यत्यय ). Even if a *tithi* happens to be of 50 *ghatis* there is every compromise available from the religious texts. It is to be pointed out that the *sannipāt* and *vyatyaya* occur in the domain of extremities of *tithis* conforming to the S.S. School also[^4]. The present analysis exposes very successfully the baselessness of the argumentations by the followers of the S.S. School.
